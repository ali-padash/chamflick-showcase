The colour key

Four colours. Each one only ever means a gender.

After a few pages the colour arrives before the article does. That is the point: you stop looking genders up and start remembering them.

A noun the app is not sure of gets no colour, only a dashed underline. A wrong colour would read as a confident answer, so it does not guess.

Same word. Three genders.

It reads the sentence, not just the word.

Some words change their meaning with their gender. A list of nouns cannot tell which one is meant; the sentence can.

Read

Your books, your scanned books, your films.

Add a PDF or an EPUB from the phone and start reading while the rest is still being coloured. Scanned books are read by OCR. Subtitles are coloured as the film plays.

Understand

Wherever the other half is, it finds it.

A German parser works through every sentence: its structure, each word's base form, the cases. That is how a verb split across a sentence is put back together before the dictionary is asked.

On your phone, and only there

No account

Nothing to sign up for. Your books stay on your phone.

No internet needed

The colouring, the OCR and the dictionary all work offline.

No guessing

Rules, a dictionary and a parser decide each colour. What they cannot decide is left uncoloured.
